### 1.4 The August 2, 2026 Deadline — What Obligations Begin

On **2 August 2026**, the majority of the EU AI Act's substantive obligations become legally enforceable. Specifically:

- **All Annex III high-risk AI system requirements enter into application** — the full Chapter III Section 2 obligations for high-risk AI systems become enforceable, including:
  - Risk management systems (Article 9)
  - Data governance (Article 10)
  - Technical documentation (Article 11)
  - Record-keeping / logging (Article 12)
  - Transparency to deployers (Article 13)
  - Human oversight (Article 14)
  - Accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity (Article 15)
  - Provider obligations including QMS and conformity assessment (Articles 16–17)
  - Deployer obligations (Article 26)
  - EU declaration of conformity (Article 47)
  - CE marking (Article 48)
  - Registration in EU database (Article 49)
  - Post-market monitoring (Article 72)
  - Incident reporting (Article 73)

- **Penalties under Article 99** for violations relating to operators and notified bodies (Articles 16, 22–24, 26, 31, 33–34, 50) become enforceable. (Note: penalties per Articles 99 and 100 already apply from 2 August 2025; however, the underlying high-risk AI system obligations triggering those penalties come into effect on 2 August 2026.)

- **GPAI Article 101 administrative fines** (for GPAI model providers) become applicable from 2 August 2026.

**Important caveat:** As of early 2026, the Commission proposed targeted amendments under the "Digital Omnibus Package" that could shift some Annex III deadlines to December 2027 if harmonized standards are not published in time. However, **no amendment has passed into law** as of this writing; **2 August 2026 remains the current legally binding date**.

### 1.5 Article 99 — Penalties (All Tiers, Exact Text)

**Source:** [EUR-Lex — Article 99](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689) | [artificialintelligenceact.eu — Article 99](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/99/)

#### Article 99(1): Framework
> *"In accordance with the terms and conditions laid down in this Regulation, Member States shall lay down the rules on penalties and other enforcement measures, which may also include warnings and non-monetary measures, applicable to infringements of this Regulation by operators, and shall take all measures necessary to ensure that they are properly and effectively implemented, thereby taking into account the guidelines issued by the Commission pursuant to Article 96. The penalties provided for shall be effective, proportionate and dissuasive. They shall take into account the interests of SMEs, including start-ups, and their economic viability."*

#### Article 99(2): Member State notification obligation
Member States must notify the Commission of penalty rules by the date of entry into application.

#### Article 99(3): Tier 1 — Prohibited AI practices (Article 5 violations)
> *"Non-compliance with the prohibition of the AI practices referred to in Article 5 shall be subject to administrative fines of up to EUR 35 000 000 or, if the offender is an undertaking, up to 7 % of its total worldwide annual turnover for the preceding financial year, **whichever is higher**."*

#### Article 99(4): Tier 2 — Operator and notified body obligations
> *"Non-compliance with any of the following provisions related to operators or notified bodies, other than those laid down in Articles 5, shall be subject to administrative fines of up to EUR 15 000 000 or, if the offender is an undertaking, up to 3 % of its total worldwide annual turnover for the preceding financial year, whichever is higher:*
> *(a) obligations of providers pursuant to Article 16;*
> *(b) obligations of authorised representatives pursuant to Article 22;*
> *(c) obligations of importers pursuant to Article 23;*
> *(d) obligations of distributors pursuant to Article 24;*
> *(e) obligations of deployers pursuant to Article 26;*
> *(f) requirements and obligations of notified bodies pursuant to Article 31, Article 33(1), (3) and (4) or Article 34;*
> *(g) transparency obligations for providers and deployers pursuant to Article 50."*

#### Article 99(5): Tier 3 — Misleading information
> *"The supply of incorrect, incomplete or misleading information to notified bodies or national competent authorities in reply to a request shall be subject to administrative fines of up to EUR 7 500 000 or, if the offender is an undertaking, up to 1 % of its total worldwide annual turnover for the preceding financial year, **whichever is higher**."*

#### Article 99(6): SME/startup cap
> *"In the case of SMEs, including start-ups, each fine referred to in this Article shall be up to the percentages or amount referred to in paragraphs 3, 4 and 5, **whichever thereof is lower**."*

#### Article 99(7): Factors in setting fines
Relevant factors include: nature, gravity, duration of infringement; number of affected persons; prior fines; company size; financial benefits gained; cooperation with authorities; degree of responsibility; whether infringement was intentional or negligent; remedial actions taken.

#### Article 99(8)–(11): Procedural provisions
Member States determine whether fines are imposed by courts or administrative bodies; annual reporting to Commission required.

#### Summary Table

| Tier | Infringement Type | Maximum Fine |
|------|-------------------|-------------|
| 1 (Art. 99(3)) | Prohibited AI practices (Art. 5) | €35M or **7% worldwide turnover**, whichever higher |
| 2 (Art. 99(4)) | Provider/deployer/notified body obligations (Arts. 16, 22–24, 26, 31, 33–34, 50) | €15M or **3% worldwide turnover**, whichever higher |
| 3 (Art. 99(5)) | False/misleading information to authorities | €7.5M or **1% worldwide turnover**, whichever higher |
| SME cap (Art. 99(6)) | All tiers | The **lower** of the amount or percentage (reverse of normal rule) |
| GPAI (Art. 101) | GPAI model provider violations | €15M or 3% worldwide turnover, whichever higher (separate regime) |

## 2. Key Articles Relevant to Autonomous Systems / AI Compliance

### Article 6 + Annex III: High-Risk AI System Classification

**Source:** [EUR-Lex — Article 6](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689) | [artificialintelligenceact.eu — Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/)

#### Article 6(1) — Safety Components of Products (Annex I pathway)
An AI system is high-risk under Article 6(1) if **both** conditions are met:
1. The AI system is **intended to be used as a safety component** of a product covered by EU harmonization legislation listed in **Annex I** (e.g., Machinery Regulation, Medical Devices Regulation, Type Approval for vehicles, Radio Equipment Directive, Aviation, Marine Equipment, Lifts, etc.)
2. That product is required to undergo **third-party conformity assessment** under those legal acts.

*Note: Article 6(1) systems have a deferred compliance deadline of **2 August 2027***.

#### Article 6(2) — Use Case Categories (Annex III pathway)
AI systems are **also** high-risk if they fall within the use cases listed in **Annex III**. These are enforceable from **2 August 2026**.

#### Article 6(3) — Safe Harbor (Not High-Risk)
An AI system listed in Annex III shall **not** be considered high-risk where it:
- Performs a narrow procedural task; or
- Improves the result of a previously completed human activity; or
- Detects decision-making patterns without replacing human assessment; or
- Performs preparatory tasks to an Annex III assessment.

**Exception:** AI systems that perform **profiling of natural persons** are **always** high-risk regardless of the safe harbor conditions.

#### Annex III: Complete List of High-Risk AI System Categories

**1. Biometrics** (where use is permitted under law):
- (a) Remote biometric identification systems (excluding simple biometric verification)
- (b) Biometric categorisation by sensitive/protected attributes
- (c) Emotion recognition systems

**2. Critical Infrastructure** — AI intended as **safety components** in:
- Management and operation of critical digital infrastructure
- Road traffic management
- Supply of water, gas, heating, or electricity
- *[See McCarter & English analysis](https://www.mccarter.com/insights/ai-in-critical-infrastructure-markets-are-smart-systems-ai-the-eu-ai-act-says-it-maybe/)*

**3. Education and vocational training** — AI for access/admission decisions, learning outcome evaluation, level assessment, prohibited behavior monitoring

**4. Employment, workers management** — AI for recruitment, selection, task allocation, performance monitoring, termination decisions

**5. Access to essential services** — AI for:
- Public benefits/healthcare eligibility assessment
- Creditworthiness / credit scoring (excluding fraud detection)
- Life and health insurance risk pricing
- Emergency call classification and dispatch prioritization

**6. Law enforcement** — AI for victim risk assessment, polygraph tools, evidence reliability evaluation, recidivism risk scoring, profiling

**7. Migration, asylum, border control** — AI for polygraph tools, risk assessment, application examination, identity recognition

**8. Administration of justice and democratic processes** — AI for judicial research/interpretation, election/referendum influence

### Article 9: Risk Management System

**Source:** [EUR-Lex — Article 9](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689) | [artificialintelligenceact.eu — Article 9](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/9/)

Article 9 mandates a **continuous, iterative risk management system** throughout the entire lifecycle of every high-risk AI system. It must be **established, implemented, documented, and maintained**.

**Article 9(2) — Required steps:**
- (a) Identify and analyse known and **reasonably foreseeable risks** to health, safety, or fundamental rights under intended purpose
- (b) Estimate and evaluate risks under intended purpose and **reasonably foreseeable misuse**
- (c) Evaluate risks arising from **post-market monitoring data** (Article 72)
- (d) Adopt appropriate, targeted **risk management measures**

**Article 9(5) — Measure hierarchy:**
1. Eliminate or reduce risks through design and development (design-first)
2. Implement mitigation/control measures for residual risks
3. Provide information per Article 13 and training to deployers

**Article 9(6)–(8) — Testing obligations:**
- High-risk AI systems must be **tested** prior to placement on market
- Testing shall be performed at any time during development, **and in any event prior to placing on market**
- Testing against **prior defined metrics and probabilistic thresholds** appropriate to intended purpose

**Practitioner note:** The risk management system must be proportionate to the context of use, including the technical knowledge and experience of the expected deployer. For autonomous systems deployed in edge environments, this creates particular challenges around foreseeable misuse scenarios (e.g., connectivity failure, sensor degradation, adversarial inputs).

**Academic reference:** Schuett, Jonas (2023). "Risk management in the Artificial Intelligence Act." *European Journal of Risk Regulation*. DOI: [10.1017/err.2023.1](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/2E4D5707E65EFB3251A76E288BA74068/S1867299X23000016a.pdf/div-class-title-risk-management-in-the-artificial-intelligence-act-div.pdf)

### Article 12: Record-Keeping / Logging Obligations

**Source:** [artificialintelligenceact.eu — Article 12](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/12/) | [artificialintelligenceact.eu — Article 19](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/19/)

#### Article 12(1) — Core obligation:
> *"High-risk AI systems shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system."*

Key parsing:
- **"Technically allow"** = design-time requirement; the capability must be built in
- **"Automatic"** = no manual triggering; logs must be generated without operator intervention at the moment events occur
- **"Lifetime"** = from deployment through decommissioning, not just current version

#### Article 12(2) — What logs must enable:
Logging capabilities must enable recording of events relevant for:
- (a) Identifying situations where the system **presents a risk** (Art. 79(1)) or undergoes **substantial modification**
- (b) Facilitating **post-market monitoring** (Art. 72)
- (c) **Monitoring** by the deployer (Art. 26(5))

#### Article 12(3) — Enhanced requirements for biometric identification systems (Annex III, point 1(a)):
Logging must provide, **at a minimum**:
- (a) Recording of the **period of each use** (start and end date/time)
- (b) The **reference database** against which input data was checked
- (c) The **input data** for which the search led to a match
- (d) The **identification of natural persons** involved in verifying results (Art. 14(5))

#### Article 19 — Provider log retention:
> *"Providers of high-risk AI systems shall keep the logs referred to in Article 12(1), automatically generated by their high-risk AI systems, to the extent such logs are under their control... for a period appropriate to the intended purpose of the high-risk AI system, of at least **six months**, unless provided otherwise in the applicable Union or national law."*

#### Article 26(6) — Deployer log retention:
> *"Deployers of high-risk AI systems shall keep the logs automatically generated by that high-risk AI system to the extent such logs are under their control, for a period appropriate to the intended purpose of the high-risk AI system, of at least **six months**..."*

Financial institutions may integrate logs into existing financial services law documentation frameworks.

### Article 13: Transparency and Provision of Information to Deployers

**Source:** [artificialintelligenceact.eu — Article 13](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/13/)

#### Article 13(1):
> *"High-risk AI systems shall be designed and developed in such a way as to ensure that their operation is sufficiently transparent to enable deployers to interpret a system's output and use it appropriately."*

#### Article 13(2)–(3): Instructions for use must include at minimum:
- (a) Provider identity and contact details
- (b) System characteristics, capabilities, and limitations:
  - (i) Intended purpose
  - (ii) Accuracy metrics, robustness, and cybersecurity performance
  - (iii) Known/foreseeable circumstances that may affect accuracy or create risks
  - (iv) Technical capabilities to explain output (where applicable)
  - (v) Performance on specific populations (where relevant)
  - (vi) Training, validation, testing data specifications (where relevant)
  - (vii) Information to enable proper interpretation of output
- (c) Predetermined changes to system and performance since initial conformity assessment
- (d) Human oversight measures (Art. 14), including tools for output interpretation
- (e) Computational and hardware resources, expected lifetime, maintenance requirements
- **(f) Description of mechanisms for deployers to collect, store, and interpret logs in accordance with Article 12**

Note: Point (f) makes the instructions for use an important technical integration guide for logging systems — providers must explain **how** deployers can implement logging.

### Article 14: Human Oversight

**Source:** [artificialintelligenceact.eu — Article 14](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/14/)

#### Article 14(1):
> *"High-risk AI systems shall be designed and developed in such a way, including with appropriate human-machine interface tools, that they can be effectively overseen by natural persons during the period in which they are in use."*

#### Article 14(2): Purpose
Human oversight aims to **prevent or minimise risks** to health, safety, or fundamental rights that persist despite other technical requirements.

#### Article 14(3): How oversight is implemented
Measures must be **commensurate with risks, level of autonomy, and context of use**, implemented through:
- (a) Built-in measures by the **provider** before market placement; OR
- (b) Measures for appropriate implementation by the **deployer**

#### Article 14(4): What overseen persons must be enabled to do:
- (a) Properly understand capacities, limitations, and **monitor operation** including detecting anomalies, dysfunctions, unexpected performance
- (b) Remain aware of risk of **automation bias** (over-reliance on AI output)
- (c) Correctly **interpret the AI system's output**
- (d) **Decide not to use** the system, or disregard, override, or reverse its output
- (e) **Intervene** in operation or **interrupt the system** through a "stop button or similar procedure that allows the system to come to a halt in a safe state"

#### Article 14(5): Enhanced requirement for biometric identification (Annex III, point 1(a)):
No action or decision shall be taken unless the identification has been **separately verified and confirmed by at least two natural persons** with necessary competence, training, and authority.

**Critical implication for autonomous systems:** The "stop button" or safe halt capability (Art. 14(4)(e)) and the human monitoring capability (Art. 14(4)(a)) must be **designed and built into** the system by the provider. For fully autonomous edge systems (drones, autonomous vehicles, industrial robots), this creates a design-level requirement for override/intervention capabilities that may conflict with operational autonomy.

### Articles 16–17: Provider Obligations

**Source:** [artificialintelligenceact.eu — Article 16](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/16/) | [artificialintelligenceact.eu — Article 17](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/17/)

#### Article 16: Core Obligations of Providers

Providers of high-risk AI systems shall:
- **(a)** Ensure compliance with Section 2 requirements (Arts. 9–15)
- **(b)** Display name/trade mark and contact address on the system, packaging, or documentation
- **(c)** Have a quality management system in place (Art. 17)
- **(d)** Keep technical documentation (Art. 18)
- **(e)** When under their control, keep automatically generated logs (Art. 19)
- **(f)** Ensure the system undergoes the relevant **conformity assessment** (Art. 43)
- **(g)** Draw up an **EU declaration of conformity** (Art. 47)
- **(h)** Affix **CE marking** (Art. 48)
- **(i)** Comply with **registration obligations** (Art. 49(1))
- **(j)** Take necessary **corrective actions** and provide required information (Art. 20)
- **(k)** Upon reasoned request of national competent authority, **demonstrate conformity**
- **(l)** Ensure compliance with **accessibility requirements** (Directives 2016/2102 and 2019/882)

#### Article 17: Quality Management System (QMS)

Providers must put a **documented, systematic QMS in place** that includes at minimum:

- **(a)** Regulatory compliance strategy, including conformity assessment procedures and management of modifications
- **(b)** Design control and verification techniques
- **(c)** Development quality control and quality assurance techniques
- **(d)** Examination, test, and validation procedures (before, during, and after development)
- **(e)** Technical specifications, including applicable standards and means to ensure compliance where harmonized standards are not fully applied
- **(f)** Data management systems: data acquisition, collection, analysis, labelling, storage, filtration, mining, aggregation, retention
- **(g)** The risk management system (Art. 9)
- **(h)** Setup, implementation, and maintenance of a **post-market monitoring system** (Art. 72)
- **(i)** Procedures for **serious incident reporting** (Art. 73)
- **(j)** Communication handling with national authorities, notified bodies, and other operators
- **(k)** Systems and procedures for **record-keeping** of all relevant documentation and information
- **(l)** Resource management, including security-of-supply measures
- **(m)** **Accountability framework** setting out responsibilities of management and other staff

**Article 17(2):** Implementation shall be **proportionate to the size** of the provider's organisation.

**Note on harmonized standard:** On 30 October 2025, **prEN 18286** (*Artificial Intelligence – Quality Management System for EU AI Act Regulatory Purposes*) became the first harmonized standard for AI to enter public enquiry, designed specifically to support Article 17 compliance. Source: [European Commission — AI Act Standardization](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-act-standardisation)

### Article 26: Deployer Obligations

**Source:** [artificialintelligenceact.eu — Article 26](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/26/)

#### Key obligations:

**26(1):** Use system **in accordance with instructions for use** (Art. 13); apply appropriate technical and organisational measures.

**26(2):** Assign human oversight to persons with **necessary competence, training, and authority**, plus necessary support.

**26(4):** To the extent the deployer controls input data, ensure data is **relevant and sufficiently representative** for the intended purpose.

**26(5):** **Monitor the operation** of the high-risk AI system; where there is reason to consider the system presents a risk (Art. 79(1)), inform the provider/distributor and relevant market surveillance authority **without undue delay** and **suspend use**. For serious incidents, immediately inform the provider first, then importer/distributor and market surveillance authorities.

**26(6):** Keep automatically generated logs for **at least six months** (unless other law requires longer).

**26(7):** Before deploying at the workplace, **inform workers' representatives and affected workers** that they will be subject to the high-risk AI system.

**26(8):** Public authority deployers must **check registration** of high-risk AI systems in EU database (Art. 71) before use; do not use unregistered systems.

**26(9):** Use Article 13 information to comply with GDPR **data protection impact assessments** (Art. 35 GDPR) where applicable.

**26(11):** Deployers making or assisting decisions related to natural persons must **inform those persons** that they are subject to a high-risk AI system.

**26(12):** Cooperate with national competent authorities in any action they take.

### Article 47: EU Declaration of Conformity

**Source:** [artificialintelligenceact.eu — Article 47](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/47/)

**Article 47(1):** The provider shall draw up a **written, machine-readable, physically or electronically signed** EU declaration of conformity for each high-risk AI system, and keep it available to national competent authorities for **10 years** after the system is placed on the market or put into service.

**Article 47(2):** Must state the system meets Section 2 requirements and contain information set out in **Annex V**. Must be translated into languages required by Member States.

**Article 47(3):** Where high-risk AI systems are also subject to other EU harmonization legislation, a **single EU declaration of conformity** covering all applicable EU law may be drawn up.

**Article 47(4):** By drawing up the declaration, the provider **assumes responsibility** for compliance. Must keep declaration up-to-date.

### Article 49: CE Marking and Registration

**Note:** Articles 48 and 49 address different topics. Article 48 covers **CE marking**; Article 49 covers **registration in the EU database**. The annotated site lists Article 49 under "Registration."

**Source:** [artificialintelligenceact.eu — Article 49](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/49/)

**Article 49(1):** Before placing on market or putting into service a high-risk AI system listed in Annex III (except point 2 — critical infrastructure), the provider shall **register** themselves and the system in the EU database (Art. 71).

**Article 49(2):** Providers who conclude their system is **not** high-risk under Art. 6(3) must **still register** themselves and the system in the EU database.

**Article 49(3):** Before deploying or using an Annex III high-risk AI system, **public authority deployers** must register themselves and register the use in the EU database.

**Article 49(4):** For high-risk systems in law enforcement, migration, and border control (Annex III, points 1, 6, 7), registration is in a **secure non-public section** of the EU database with restricted access.

**CE Marking (Article 48):** The CE marking must be affixed to the high-risk AI system or, where not possible, on its packaging or documentation, to indicate conformity with the Regulation. The CE marking provisions of Regulation (EC) No 765/2008 apply. The CE marking is **visible, legible, and indelible**.

## 3. Enforcement Architecture

### 3.1 National Market Surveillance Authorities (MSAs)

**Source:** [European Commission — Market Surveillance Authorities under the AI Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/market-surveillance-authorities-under-ai-act) | [European Commission — Governance and Enforcement](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-act-governance-and-enforcement)

Each EU Member State must designate **one or more market surveillance authorities** (MSAs), with the designation deadline of **2 August 2025**. If a Member State has multiple MSAs, it must designate a **Single Point of Contact** for public and cross-border coordination.

**MSA powers:**
- Investigate and enforce compliance with AI Act rules, including prohibitions and high-risk AI rules
- Conduct **remote monitoring**
- **Access providers' documentation, data sets, and source code**
- Propose joint investigations with the Commission
- Request corrective measures
- Enforce through imposition of penalties
- **Annual reporting** to the Commission and relevant national authorities on measures adopted and risk evaluations

**MSAs must be:** independent, impartial, unbiased; have adequate technical, financial, and human resources.

**Cooperation mechanism:** MSAs come together under the **European AI Board** framework for cross-border cooperation, information exchange, and consistent enforcement. Existing EU tools for information exchange and cooperation (from Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 on market surveillance) are available.

**EDPS as EU-institutions MSA:** The European Data Protection Supervisor (EDPS) is the designated MSA for EU institutions, bodies, and agencies.

**Notifying authorities** are separate from MSAs — they designate and supervise **notified bodies** (third-party conformity assessment bodies). Both MSAs and notifying authorities are collectively referred to as **national competent authorities** under the AI Act.

### 3.2 The European AI Office

**Source:** [European Commission — European AI Office](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-office)

The European AI Office was established **within the European Commission** as:
- The **centre of AI expertise** across the EU
- The **foundation for a single European AI governance system**
- **Responsible for enforcing rules for general-purpose AI (GPAI) models**

**Organisational structure:**
- 6 units: Excellence in AI and Robotics; Regulation and Compliance; AI Safety; AI Innovation and Policy Coordination; AI for Societal Good; AI in Health and Life Science
- 2 advisors: Lead Scientific Advisor; Advisor for International Affairs
- 125+ staff (technology specialists, lawyers, policy specialists, economists)

**Enforcement powers (GPAI models):**
- Conduct **evaluations** of general-purpose AI models
- **Request information and measures** from model providers
- **Apply sanctions** (Art. 101 fines: up to €15M or 3% worldwide turnover)
- In November 2025, the Commission proposed additional targeted amendments (**Digital Simplification Package**) to reinforce the AI Office's powers and **centralise oversight of AI systems built on GPAI models**

**Advisory governance bodies** working with the AI Office:
1. **European Artificial Intelligence Board** — composed of Member State representatives
2. **Scientific Panel** — independent AI experts
3. **Advisory Forum** — diverse commercial and non-commercial stakeholders

### 3.3 How Conformity Assessments Work

**Source:** [artificialintelligenceact.eu — Article 43](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/43/) | [FPF — Conformity Assessments Guide](https://fpf.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/OT-comformity-assessment-under-the-eu-ai-act-WP-1.pdf)

The type of conformity assessment depends on the **category** of high-risk AI system:

#### Annex III, point 1 (Biometrics) — Article 43(1):
Two options:
- **(a) Internal control** (Annex VI — self-assessment) — if using harmonized standards
- **(b) Third-party assessment** by a **notified body** (Annex VII — quality management system + technical documentation assessment) — required if harmonized standards not applied in full, or if system is post-remote biometric identification

#### Annex III, points 2–8 — Article 43(2):
**Self-assessment only** (internal control per Annex VI). No third-party notified body involvement required.

#### Annex I products (safety components in regulated products) — Article 43(3):
Follow the **sector-specific conformity assessment** required by the applicable harmonization legislation (e.g., Machinery Regulation, MDR, vehicle type-approval). The AI Act's Section 2 requirements must be incorporated into that assessment. Notified bodies under those sector laws may assess AI Act conformity.

#### Notified Bodies:
- Must be **accredited** by a national accreditation body
- Must apply for notification to the national notifying authority
- Must meet requirements of Article 31 (independence, competence, resources)
- Notified bodies from third countries may be authorised if a bilateral agreement exists (Art. 39)

#### Key process steps:
1. Determine whether conformity assessment is required (is system high-risk?)
2. Select applicable conformity assessment procedure (internal vs. third-party)
3. Apply harmonized standards where available (confers presumption of conformity)
4. If no harmonized standards: demonstrate compliance through other means (common specifications, technical files)
5. Draw up **EU declaration of conformity** (Art. 47)
6. Affix **CE marking** (Art. 48)
7. **Register** in EU database (Art. 49)

**Important caveat:** As of this writing, **no harmonized standard for AI Act purposes has been published in the Official Journal of the EU**. Companies cannot yet rely on harmonized standards to trigger the presumption of conformity; they must demonstrate compliance through direct evidence. Source: [ISMS.online — ISO 42001 and harmonized standards](https://www.isms.online/frameworks/iso-42001/iso-42001-harmonised-standards-eu-ai-act-presumption-of-conformity/)

### 3.4 Post-Market Monitoring Obligations

**Source:** [artificialintelligenceact.eu — Article 72](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/72/) | [Naaia.ai — Post-market monitoring](https://naaia.ai/post-market-monitoring-an-important-ai-act-requirement/)

#### Article 72(1):
> *"Providers shall establish and document a post-market monitoring system in a manner that is proportionate to the nature of the AI technologies and the risks of the high-risk AI system."*

#### Article 72(2) — What the system must do:
**Actively and systematically** collect, document, and analyse relevant data to evaluate **continuous compliance** with Chapter III Section 2 requirements throughout the system's lifetime. Where relevant, includes analysis of **interactions with other AI systems**.

#### Article 72(3) — Post-market monitoring plan:
Must be **part of the technical documentation** (Annex IV). The Commission adopted an implementing act to establish a template for the plan **by 2 February 2026**.

#### Article 72 — Integration with Annex I products:
Where post-market surveillance already required under sector law, providers may **integrate** AI Act requirements into existing systems and plans.

**Data that must be collected:** Performance data; usage patterns; incidents or near-incidents; data enabling assessment of whether the system continues to meet accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity, and non-discrimination requirements.

**Relationship to Article 12 logs:** The post-market monitoring system feeds directly on the logs required by Article 12 — the logs must enable "facilitating the post-market monitoring referred to in Article 72" (Art. 12(2)(b)).

### 4.2 Logging / Record-Keeping Requirements — Detail

**Sources:** [FireTail — Article 12 analysis](https://www.firetail.ai/blog/article-12-and-the-logging-mandate-what-the-eu-ai-act-actually-requires) | [ISMS.online — Article 12 compliance](https://www.isms.online/iso-42001/eu-ai-act/article-12/) | [Practical AI Act Guide](https://practical-ai-act.eu/latest/conformity/record-keeping/)

#### What must be stored:

| Log Element | Regulatory Basis |
|------------|-----------------|
| Session ID / use period (start/end timestamps) | Art. 12(3)(a); Art. 12(2) |
| Input data / data version used | Art. 12(2)(a) |
| Reference database checked (biometric systems) | Art. 12(3)(b) |
| Input data leading to a match (biometric systems) | Art. 12(3)(c) |
| Identity of human verifiers (biometric systems) | Art. 12(3)(d) / Art. 14(5) |
| Events indicating potential risk or substantial modification | Art. 12(2)(a) |
| Post-market monitoring data | Art. 12(2)(b) |
| Deployer monitoring events | Art. 12(2)(c) |
| Human override / intervention events | Art. 14(4)(e) (implicit) |
| Model version / configuration at inference | Best practice; supports conformity demonstration |

#### How long:
- **Minimum: 6 months** (Art. 19 for providers; Art. 26(6) for deployers)
- **Longer** if other Union or national law requires it (e.g., GDPR, financial services law)
- **Technical documentation:** must be kept for **10 years** after market placement (Art. 47(1))

#### Format:
The AI Act does **not prescribe a specific format**. However:
- Logs must be **automatically generated** (not manually compiled)
- Logs must be **interpretable** by the deployer and by regulatory authorities
- Article 13(3)(f) requires providers to explain in instructions how deployers can "collect, store and interpret the logs"
- **Tamper-evidence** is increasingly treated as a de facto requirement by practitioners (see section 6)

#### Retention rules for financial institutions:
Both providers (Art. 19) and deployers (Art. 26(6)) that are financial institutions subject to Union financial services law may integrate AI logs into existing documentation kept under that law.

### 4.3 Audit Trail Requirements — What Auditors Will Need to Verify

**Source:** [Wolters Kluwer — Internal Audit and EU AI Act](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/expert-insights/innovation-regulation-how-internal-audit-must-respond-eu-ai-act) | [VeritasChain — Cryptographic Audit Trails](https://veritaschain.org/blog/posts/2025-12-25-eu-ai-act-cryptographic-audit/)

An EU AI Act audit (internal or third-party) will require verification of:

| Domain | What Auditors Verify |
|--------|---------------------|
| **AI Inventory** | Complete, current register of all AI systems in scope; risk classification justified and documented |
| **Risk Management** | Documented risk management system per Art. 9; evidence of testing against defined metrics |
| **Logging/Records** | Technical evidence that automatic logging is in place; 6-month retention verified; log integrity (tamper-evidence) |
| **Human Oversight** | Named oversight persons with demonstrated competence; stop/override capability; evidence of override events logged |
| **Data Governance** | Training/validation/testing data documented per Art. 10; input data relevance/representativeness controls |
| **Technical Documentation** | Complete technical file (Annex IV); kept up to date; EU declaration of conformity |
| **Post-Market Monitoring** | Active monitoring plan; evidence of data collection; anomaly detection; incident reporting procedures |
| **Conformity Assessment** | Correct procedure followed per Art. 43; CE marking affixed; registration in EU database confirmed |
| **Third-Party Risk** | Vendor AI systems covered; contractual protections; transparency obligations flowing down supply chain |
| **Incident Response** | Documented procedures; evidence of actual reporting to provider and MSA for past incidents |

**Key risk for auditors:** Logs that exist but are **mutable** (can be edited or deleted without detection) may not satisfy Art. 73(6)'s implicit prohibition on evidence alteration. Auditors should test for tamper-evidence, not just log existence.

### 4.4 The Specific Challenge for Edge AI / Autonomous Systems / IoT

**Sources:** [STL Partners — Edge AI and EU AI Act](https://stlpartners.com/articles/edge-computing/inside-the-eu-ai-act/) | [Digital Watch Observatory — Edge AI challenges](https://dig.watch/updates/edge-ai-advantages-and-challenges-shaping-the-future-of-digital-systems) | [VerifyWise — Edge AI Governance](https://verifywise.ai/lexicon/edge-ai-governance)

The EU AI Act was primarily drafted with **centralized AI systems** in mind. Edge AI, autonomous systems, and IoT deployments face a set of structural challenges:

| Challenge | AI Act Requirement | Edge/Autonomous Problem |
|-----------|-------------------|------------------------|
| **Automatic logging** (Art. 12(1)) | Must log automatically, continuously | Edge devices have limited compute/storage; intermittent connectivity prevents real-time upload |
| **Lifetime logging** (Art. 12(1)) | Logs from deployment to decommissioning | Device may be physically inaccessible; storage capacity finite; firmware updates may disrupt log continuity |
| **Tamper-evidence** (Art. 73(6) implicit) | Logs must not be alterable | Edge devices controlled by end-users/deployers; local storage is modifiable; physical access risk |
| **6-month retention** (Art. 19, 26(6)) | ≥6 months of logs | Device storage may be insufficient; connectivity for cloud backup unreliable |
| **Human oversight** (Art. 14) | "Stop button or similar procedure" | Fully autonomous systems (drones, AVs, industrial robots) may have no real-time human connection |
| **Post-market monitoring** (Art. 72) | Active collection of performance data | Edge deployment is distributed across thousands of devices; aggregation is a significant engineering problem |
| **Conformity after modification** | Substantial modifications require reassessment | OTA (over-the-air) updates may constitute substantial modifications; provider bears ongoing responsibility |
| **Deployer control of logs** | Deployers must keep logs "to the extent such logs are under their control" | On-device logs may be under the deployer's physical control but not their technical control (locked firmware) |

**Industry assessment:** "Existing regulatory frameworks have been largely designed for centralised systems and do not fully address the complexities of decentralised architectures. Questions regarding liability, accountability, and enforcement remain unresolved." — [Digital Watch Observatory](https://dig.watch/updates/edge-ai-advantages-and-challenges-shaping-the-future-of-digital-systems)

**Practical consequence:** The phrase "to the extent such logs are under their control" in Articles 19 and 26(6) provides a partial escape valve — but the qualifier does not eliminate the obligation; it only recognises that in some deployments, providers or deployers cannot guarantee access to every log. This creates **significant legal uncertainty** for edge AI operators about how much of this obligation can be discharged.

#### ETSI TS 104 008 — Continuous Auditing-Based Conformity Assessment

**Source:** [ETSI — TS 104 008 press release](https://www.etsi.org/newsroom/press-releases/2634-etsi-launches-new-standard-enabling-continuous-compliance-for-dynamic-ai-systems-aligned-with-the-eu-ai-act)

Published **27 January 2026**, ETSI TS 104 008 introduces **Continuous Auditing-Based Conformity Assessment (CABCA)**:

- Supports the AI Act's **post-market monitoring obligations** (Art. 72)
- Addresses the problem that traditional point-in-time audits are insufficient for **dynamic AI systems** that evolve through updates, retraining, and new data
- Provides methodology for **translating abstract legal requirements into verifiable, continuously monitored metrics**
- Enables **continuous evidence collection and automated reporting**
- Operationalizes **real-time non-conformity detection**
- Covers full scope: quality, safety, security, transparency, governance

*This standard is directly relevant to autonomous systems and edge AI deployments where lifecycle compliance is continuous rather than point-in-time.*

## 6. Blockchain and Compliance

### 6.1 Official EU Documents Mentioning Blockchain for AI Compliance

No official EU document (Commission Regulation, AI Office guidance, or Council/Parliament text) explicitly mandates blockchain for AI compliance or audit trails. However:

- **Indirect support via eIDAS 2.0** (see section 6.2): The Commission has created legal status for **qualified electronic ledgers** (blockchain-compatible) under Regulation (EU) 2024/1183, and issued implementing acts specifying technical standards.

- **Article 73(6) of the AI Act** (implicit prohibition on evidence tampering): *"The provider shall cooperate with the competent authorities and shall not perform any investigation which involves altering the AI system concerned in a way which may affect any subsequent evaluation of the causes of the incident..."* This implicitly prohibits **evidence tampering** and creates a de facto requirement that audit logs be tamper-evident, even though the mechanism (blockchain or otherwise) is not specified. Source: [VeritasChain — Cryptographic Audit Trails Analysis](https://veritaschain.org/blog/posts/2025-12-25-eu-ai-act-cryptographic-audit/)

- **ETSI TS 104 008** (January 2026): While not explicitly blockchain-focused, this standard for Continuous Auditing-Based Conformity Assessment creates the technical framework within which blockchain-based logging can operate as a preferred implementation. Source: [ETSI — TS 104 008](https://www.etsi.org/newsroom/press-releases/2634-etsi-launches-new-standard-enabling-continuous-compliance-for-dynamic-ai-systems-aligned-with-the-eu-ai-act)

### 6.2 eIDAS 2.0 — Regulation (EU) 2024/1183 and Qualified Trust Services

**Source:** [EUR-Lex — Regulation (EU) 2024/1183](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024R1183) | [EY — eIDAS 2.0 Analysis](https://www.ey.com/en_gr/technical/tax/tax-alerts/ey-regulation-eu-2024-1183-the-new-framework-for-a-european-digital-identity)

Regulation (EU) 2024/1183 (eIDAS 2.0, published 30 April 2024) amends the original eIDAS Regulation and introduces **new categories of qualified trust services**, including:

#### Qualified Electronic Ledgers (Article 45k–45l)

**Definition:**
> *"Electronic ledger" means a sequence of electronic data records, ensuring the integrity of those records and the accuracy of the chronological ordering of those records.* (Art. 3(52))

> *"Qualified electronic ledger" means an electronic ledger which is provided by a qualified trust service provider and which meets the requirements laid down in Article 45l.* (Art. 3(53))

**Legal effect (Article 45k):**
Data records in a qualified electronic ledger enjoy a **legal presumption of**:
- Unique and accurate sequential chronological ordering
- Integrity of those records

This creates a **reversal of the burden of proof** — the records are presumed authentic and tamper-proof, which is legally significant for regulatory audit trails.

**Cross-border recognition:** Qualified electronic ledgers provided in one Member State are **recognised as qualified in all other Member States** (Art. 24a(11)).

**Technology neutrality:** eIDAS 2.0 is explicitly technology-neutral — it does not mandate blockchain specifically, but blockchain-based systems can qualify if they meet the requirements. The Commission's implementing acts specify technical standards (to be adopted by 21 May 2025 per Art. 45l(3)).

#### Qualified Timestamping

Under eIDAS, **qualified electronic timestamps**:
- Are cross-border recognised in all Member States
- Bind data to a precise UTC-synchronized time
- Are electronically signed by the Qualified Trust Service Provider (QTSP)
- Create a presumption of integrity and accurate date of the data at the time of timestamping
- Must comply with ETSI EN 319 421 and related standards

**Connection to AI Act compliance:**

| eIDAS Capability | AI Act Provision Supported |
|-----------------|--------------------------|
| Qualified electronic ledger | Art. 12(1) — tamper-evident lifetime logging |
| Qualified timestamp | Art. 12(3)(a) — recording periods of use; Art. 18(1) — 10-year retention of declaration of conformity |
| Qualified electronic signature | Art. 47(1) — electronically signed EU declaration of conformity |
| Electronic archiving | Art. 18/19 — long-term preservation of technical documentation and logs |

Source: [BSI — AI and eIDAS 2.0](https://www.bsigroup.com/en-GB/insights-and-media/insights/blogs/operating-in-a-new-trust-framework-ai-and-eidas20/) | [Evidency — Qualified Timestamping](https://evidency.io/en/qualified-timestamping/)

#### Practical Architecture (Blockchain + eIDAS)

The legally sound architecture for EU AI Act compliance using blockchain and eIDAS:

```
[Edge Device / AI System]
    ↓ Raw events (auto-generated, Art. 12)
[On-device hashing layer]
    ↓ SHA-256 / Ed25519 hash of event
[Off-chain encrypted storage] — GDPR-compliant; erasure possible
    ↓ Merkle root / hash chain
[Immutable ledger layer] — Blockchain or qualified electronic ledger
    ↓ Anchored hash + metadata
[Qualified Electronic Timestamp (QTSP)] — eIDAS qualified; ETSI EN 319 421
    ↓ Timestamped proof
[Audit-ready evidence package] — Verifiable by regulators, courts
```

**Key principle:** Personal data never goes on-chain; only **cryptographic hashes** are anchored. When GDPR erasure requests arrive, the encryption key for the off-chain data is destroyed (crypto-shredding), while the on-chain hash (containing no personal data) remains intact — preserving the audit trail. Source: [Blockchain Council — Blockchain for AI Compliance](https://www.blockchain-council.org/blockchain/blockchain-for-ai-compliance-gdpr-hipaa-eu-ai-act-immutable-logs/) | [VeritasChain — GDPR and cryptographic audit trails](https://veritaschain.org/blog/posts/2025-12-25-eu-ai-act-cryptographic-audit/)

### 6.3 Standards Bodies Working on Blockchain for Compliance

#### CEN/TS 18264:2026 — Policy and Security Requirements on Trust Services on Electronic Ledgers

**Source:** [Iteh Standards — CEN/TS 18264:2026](https://standards.iteh.ai/articles/blog/latest-news/electronic-ledger-standards-mar-2026)

Published **March 2026** by CEN, this technical specification:
- Provides **policy, functional, and security requirements** for qualified trust services operating on electronic ledgers (including blockchains and distributed ledgers)
- Aligns with **eIDAS 2 (Regulation (EU) 2024/1183)**
- Mandates:
  - Cryptographic integrity via hash chains
  - Authenticated, authorized identities for all ledger interactions
  - Consensus mechanisms for consistent data ordering
  - Detection and logging of anomalies and unauthorized access
  - Integration with timestamping and preservation services per **ETSI TS 119 511**
  - Qualified timestamping per **ETSI EN 319 421** where strict temporal accuracy is required
- Organizations aligning with CEN/TS 18264:2026 demonstrate compliance with eIDAS 2 and are positioned for EU-wide recognition of their trust services

#### ISO/TC 307 — Blockchain and Distributed Ledger Technologies

**Source:** [Nethermind — ISO TC 307](https://www.nethermind.io/blog/formal-verification-the-security-baseline-for-ethereums-proof-driven-future)

ISO/TC 307 Working Group 4 (WG4) focuses on **data governance, privacy, and data minimization** in blockchain systems — directly relevant to the tension between immutable blockchain logs and GDPR erasure rights. CEN JTC 19 (European equivalent) is the EU counterpart body.

#### ETSI Standards for Timestamping (AI Act relevance)

- **ETSI EN 319 421** — Policy and Security Requirements for Trust Service Providers issuing Time-Stamps (the qualified timestamp standard)
- **ETSI TS 119 511** — Policy and security requirements for trust service providers providing long-term preservation of digital signatures or general data
- **ETSI EN 319 401/411** — General Policy Requirements for Trust Service Providers (including signatures); these are referenced in eIDAS conformity assessment requirements

#### CEN-CENELEC JTC 21 — Record-Keeping Standard (in development)

CEN-CENELEC JTC 21 is developing a harmonized standard specifically for **record-keeping** (one of the 10 mandated areas). Until published, the record-keeping standard for Art. 12 compliance remains under development.
