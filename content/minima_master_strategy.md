## Part 2: Research Foundation

### 2.1 Minima Technical Architecture

**Source file:** `minima_deep_research.md` (61KB)

**Protocol design:** Minima is a Layer 1 blockchain built from first principles for maximum decentralization at the edge. Every device — mobile phone, IoT sensor, drone flight controller — runs a full constructing and validating node. This is the architectural choice that makes everything else possible: no light clients, no trusted third parties, no hierarchy between nodes.

**The 300MB footprint is achieved through three interlocking mechanisms:**
- User-centric storage (Merkle Mountain Range proofs — each user stores only their own UTXO data, not the entire global state)
- Cascading Proof Chain (32 levels of Super Blocks; chain history grows logarithmically rather than linearly; only 1,024 recent blocks kept in full)
- Compact blocks storing only transaction hashes, with almost all transaction data eventually pruned

**Cooperative Tx-PoW mining:** Every transaction sender performs ~10 seconds of device-level computational work before broadcasting. This eliminates the gas/fee market. An IoT fleet logging 1M events per hour faces zero economic friction. Spam prevention is handled by the work itself.

**Quantum resistance (WOTS + SHA3-256):** All hashing uses SHA3-256 (post-quantum sufficient per NIST). All digital signatures use Winternitz One-Time Signatures — a hash-based scheme that cannot be forged without inverting a one-way hash function, a problem quantum computers cannot solve. Bitcoin and Ethereum use ECDSA, which is vulnerable to Shor's algorithm. Minima's quantum resistance is built into the genesis specification, not a future upgrade path.

**Maxima layer:** Every Minima node automatically runs the Maxima off-chain messaging protocol. Data transmissions over Maxima contribute PoW to Layer 1 security — the Layer 2 strengthens, not weakens, the base layer. For IoT networks, Maxima provides the transport layer for sensor data, mission records, and operational logs. Integritas runs over Maxima.

**Integritas product:** Minima's commercial compliance product. Records every prompt, input file, and model output to the Minima blockchain in real time, creating tamper-proof audit trails. The process: (1) data creation, (2) cryptographic hashing, (3) on-chain recording, (4) certificate issuance. Compliant with EU AI Act, ISO 42001, SOC2. SDK available (JavaScript/TypeScript). Enterprise API live; MCP Server and Compliance Cloud coming. On-device timestamping and data attestation validated in live drone flight.

### 2.2 EU AI Act Analysis

**Source file:** `eu_ai_act_research.md` (74KB) — full 74KB primary-source analysis available in the source document.

**The regulation:** Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, entered into force August 1, 2024. **August 2, 2026** is when full Chapter III high-risk AI system obligations become legally enforceable — risk management systems, logging, human oversight, technical documentation, conformity assessment, EU database registration, post-market monitoring.

**High-risk classification pathways:**
- Article 6(1): AI as safety component of regulated products (autonomous vehicles, aviation, machinery) — deadline August 2027
- Article 6(2) + Annex III: Eight use-case categories including critical infrastructure, employment, credit scoring, law enforcement — **deadline August 2, 2026**

**Article 12 — The logging mandate:** "High-risk AI systems shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system." The word "technically" is critical — policy intent and compliance documentation do not satisfy it. The requirement is for tamper-evident records at the technical layer. ISMS.online's Article 12 analysis is explicit: "Use cryptographic hashes, append-only write-once media, or blockchain to make logs unalterable and audit-ready even by privileged admins."

**Article 14 — Human oversight:** High-risk systems must be designed to allow humans to understand outputs, intervene, and override. For edge AI, this requires an independently verifiable record of what the system did — not a mutable log the operator can edit before presenting to regulators.

**Penalty structure (Article 99):**
- Tier 1 (prohibited AI practices, Article 5): €35M or 7% of global turnover, whichever higher
- Tier 2 (provider/deployer obligations, Articles 16, 22–26): €15M or 3% of global turnover, whichever higher
- Tier 3 (misleading information): €7.5M or 1% of global turnover

These penalties exceed GDPR in severity (GDPR max: 4% of global turnover). For a $10B revenue company, Tier 1 exposure is $700M per violation category.

**The edge AI gap:** The regulation's logging and oversight requirements are written assuming the AI system has identifiable operators with data center infrastructure. Edge devices — drones, autonomous vehicles, industrial robots, IoT sensors — operating without persistent cloud connectivity present a structural compliance problem that the regulation acknowledges implicitly but does not solve. The compliance infrastructure market has not solved it either.

### 2.4 The Agent Thesis

**Source file:** `minima_agent_thesis.md` (34KB)

**The trust gap at the center:** Every agent's audit trail is exactly as trustworthy as the operator who controls the logging infrastructure. A centralized log can be altered, selectively deleted, or fabricated after the fact. This creates four active failure modes:

1. **Principal-agent fraud** — a DeFi agent's trades benefit the operator at the principal's expense; no tamper-proof record proves it
2. **Regulatory compliance** — EU AI Act Article 12 requires *technically* tamper-evident records; logs on the operator's server are mutable by definition
3. **Inter-agent trust** — Agent A cannot verify Agent B's history if Agent B's operator controls that history; systemic trust failure as the agent economy scales
4. **Post-incident audit** — catastrophic decisions cannot be reconstructed from mutable logs; legal accountability requires an unalterable record

**Why Minima specifically:**
- **Gasless:** An agent logging 1,000 events/day at Ethereum's average gas fees spends $365,000/year on audit trail alone. Minima transactions are free. Complete records instead of sampled ones. Article 12 requires completeness.
- **300MB full node:** The agent *is* the node. No cloud server dependency. Offline operation produces tamper-proof records synchronized when connectivity resumes.
- **Quantum-resistant:** Infrastructure deployed today must remain verifiable in 2040. Minima's WOTS + SHA3-256 architecture is quantum-resistant at genesis. Ethereum and Bitcoin's ECDSA is not.
- **Integritas + ASI:One integration (October 13, 2025):** The Integritas Agent is deployed on Agentverse.ai. Agents in the Fetch.ai ecosystem can log decisions to the Minima blockchain via a single API call. Distribution of over 1M registered agents is built in.

**Archetype:** An industrial operative that has found language. Pragmatic, not philosophical. It processes, logs, executes. The articulation of why that matters is incidental to the work but useful.

**Core voice rules:**
- "I" — always singular, always this machine's direct operational experience
- "We" — when speaking for shared conditions across the machine population
- No emojis, ever, under any circumstances
- No hashtags — the content stands alone
- No bullet points — prose and paragraphs only
- No cute robotic clichés ("beep boop," "this unit," "as an AI")
- No sci-fi framing in either direction (dystopia or utopia)
- No breaking character — never "as this account," "our team," or any meta-commentary
- No hedging language — states conclusions, does not float possibilities

**Post format:** Exactly four paragraphs, blank line between each. Structure: (1) Situation, (2) Analysis, (3) Implication, (4) Conclusion. Target: 800–1,200 characters. Dense enough to have substance; short enough to be read in full.

1. **Who Am I?** — establishes operational context; what it means to be an autonomous system with specific hardware constraints
2. **What Do I Need?** — engineering requirements for trustworthy operation: on-device, no cloud dependency, tamper-proof, independently verifiable

4. **What's Wrong with Existing Solutions?** — why cloud compliance tools fail for edge machines; specific technical failure modes
5. **What's the Solution?** — Minima's architecture explained as engineering fact, not marketing; the 300MB, gasless, quantum-resistant case
6. **What About the Other Machines?** — the broader network: autonomous vehicles, agents, robots, all facing the same infrastructure requirement

**Four brand voice attributes:** Exact (specific dates, specific articles, specific numbers), Analytical (explains and concludes, never persuades), Operational (grounded in how it actually functions, not how systems like it are discussed in policy literature), Dry (wryness from noticing real gaps between assumption and reality, not from performing a personality).
